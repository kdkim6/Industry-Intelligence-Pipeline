Customer Loyalty Scorer (NPS Proxy)

Converts public 1–5 star customer reviews into a defensible NPS-style loyalty proxy for Chipotle Mexican Grill and four competitors. The tool separates the scoring rule from the review source, makes the 4-star judgment explicit, and shows sensitivity immediately.

Method

1. Load reviews
Use the current Trustpilot benchmark or paste/upload raw public reviews.
2. Bucket ratings
5 = Promoter, 4 = Passive, 1–3 = Detractor.
3. Calculate NPS
NPS = % Promoters − % Detractors. Scores are rounded to whole numbers.
4. Test judgment
Move 4-star reviews to Promoter and show how much each score changes.
Selection-bias limitation: Public review data are self-selected and bimodal. Customers are more likely to review after unusually positive or unusually negative experiences, so a review-derived NPS proxy likely overstates how polarized the broader customer base is.

Why 4 stars are treated as Passive

A four-star review signals satisfaction, but not the customer's strongest possible endorsement. Keeping four-star reviewers in the Passive bucket preserves the conceptual distinction between a satisfied customer and an enthusiastic advocate. Because that boundary is a judgment call, the tool also recalculates every score with four-star reviews moved into Promoter so the effect is visible rather than hidden.

Survey instrument

Primary NPS question: On a scale from 0 to 10, how likely are you to recommend Chipotle to a friend or colleague?

Traditional NPS buckets: 9–10 = Promoter; 7–8 = Passive; 0–6 = Detractor.

Diagnostic follow-up: What is the primary reason for the score you gave?

Administration: Invite a random or systematically sampled set of recent customers after a neutral delay rather than surveying only highly engaged loyalty members or customers responding after a service-recovery event.

How many responses before the number means anything?

Conservative required responses: 385

Approximate worst-case margin with only 40 responses: ±31 NPS points

Conservative formula: n = (1.96 × 100 ÷ desired margin)^2. This uses a worst-case variance bound for an NPS difference of two multinomial proportions.

Interpretation

Use caution: The absolute review-derived scores should not be treated as equivalent to a probability-sampled customer survey. Their strongest use here is a transparent, consistently calculated comparison across the five firms on the same public platform.
